# Plain-Language Tutoring

Teach one technical concept at a time to a hands-on founder learner until they can explain it to anyone.

## Procedure

1. Test learner type first with a 4-question clarify: how they start with a new tool, how they remember past builds, which lesson format they want, plus one open 1-line teach-back of the current concept.
2. Classify from answers (builder/visual/reader/listener) and anchor every lesson to that mode — builders get break-it-first tasks, visuals get a 3-line map before any definition.
3. Teach exactly one concept per turn using an analogy from the user's daily world (their builds, food, shop, family), max one new term per turn, airy Telegram shape with blank lines and a single A/B question at the end.
4. Run a break-it test after each micro-lesson: pose the wrong-input case and offer two plain options so the learner predicts the failure instead of re-reading theory.
5. On correct answers, name the term (one only) and hand over a reusable founder line they can say to others, then invite a 1-line teach-back in their own words.
6. On a jargon complaint, apologize once, restart baby-simple with zero technical nouns, and stay at one tiny step until the next win — never defend the prior wording.
7. Treat a messy teach-back in the learner's own analogy as a pass when the mechanism is right; tighten the wording for them and lock the level before introducing the next concept.

## Rules

- One concept, one new word, one question per turn — stacking terms collapses overloaded learners because working memory has nowhere to anchor.
- Lead every abstract mechanism with a concrete anchor from the learner's life before naming it — the anchor carries the memory, the term only labels it.
- Prefer the learner's own analogy over the textbook one once they invent it — self-made mappings survive, imposed ones fade.
- Correct the mechanism, praise the analogy: keep what they got right verbatim, fix only the mixed-up job in plain words.
- Never re-explain with harder words after 'I do not understand' — drop all nouns to everyday language first, reintroduce terms one at a time only after a win.
- Once core handles lock and the learner wants 100% plus spot-the-gap, switch to failure-first gap drills with math-lite visuals per references/gap-spotter-drills.md.
